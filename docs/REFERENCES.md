## Notes on individual entries

**[7c] Saigo (2007) — PARTIAL, not read.** *Survey Methodology* 33(1), 61–68, "Mean-adjusted
bootstrap for two-phase sampling"; bibliographic record verified. Cited in §2.4 only as a further two-phase
source. **We have not read it.** It has been reported to reproduce Sitter's p.781 variance formula;
**that correspondence is unverified** — the published abstract does not state it — and §2.4 says so
explicitly. No claim in the paper rests on it. Its published abstract describes a bootstrap variance
procedure for two-phase designs with high sampling fractions, illustrated on the ratio estimator
and on estimators of the distribution function and quantiles; that abstract does not itself state
the p.781 correspondence, which is recorded here on the instruction of the project lead and should
be checked against the primary source.

**[7b] Roy & Safiquzzaman (2006) — read, and it does NOT review Sitter (1997).** This is the
*Journal of Official Statistics* jackknife paper that §2.4 of the manuscript refers to. Read in
full. It cites **Rao & Sitter (1995)** — the two-phase *ratio* estimator — five times, and does
**not** discuss Sitter (1997), the two-phase *regression* estimator. **The manuscript's §2.4
therefore overstates the basis for its characterisation of Sitter (1997)**, which in fact rests on
Fuller (1998) alone. Recorded 2026-08-25; the manuscript sentence has not been changed and needs
to be. The paper does corroborate the separate point that this literature builds intervals with a
**normal** quantile: the strings "Student" and "t-distribution" do not occur in it.

**[3c] Zrnic (2025) — read in full.** Algorithm 1 (PPBoot): resample the labelled data and the
unlabelled pool with replacement, form
`θ*_b = θ̂(X̃*, f(X̃*)) + θ̂(X*, Y*) − θ̂(X*, f(X*))`, and take the percentile interval. §4.2 defines
cross-PPBoot for the case where **no pre-trained model exists** and one must be trained.

**[3d] Zrnic & Candès (2023) — bibliographic verification only.** Cited by Zrnic (2025) as the
source of the cross-fitting device that cross-PPBoot adapts. Not read.

**[3e] Efron (2026) — read in full.** Proposes PPboot1 (binary responses, via a GLM on the logits
of the predictions) and **PPboot2** (quantitative responses, via the heteroskedastic linear model
`y = m(f) + s(f)·ε`), both model-based bootstraps that refit on each replicate and report
`θ̂ ± quantile × sd`. §4 treats `θ = E{y}` specifically. **An earlier note in this project stated
that no Efron PPBoot method existed; that was wrong** — it was inferred from Zrnic's reference list
rather than from a search, and is corrected here.

**[3f] Lee & Kim (2026) — abstract only.** Frames PPI as M-estimation and establishes consistency,
asymptotic normality and semiparametric efficiency. **Asymptotic**, so it does not bear on the
finite-sample interval question; recorded to keep the 2026 literature sweep complete.

**[3] Mani et al. (2025) — PARTIAL.** Abstract and retrieved sections read; the proofs were not.
`docs/prior_art_findings.md` records "`VERIFIED` for retrieved sections, `PARTIAL` for proofs",
and §2.2 characterises the paper's content, so `PARTIAL` is the honest label. Its access status was
corrected from "verified" on 2026-08-25.

**[3b] Eyre & Madras (2025) — read in full.** PDF retrieved and read (20 pages). Cited for the OLS
interpretation of `λ`, for the few-label estimation behaviour of PPI++, and for Ridge-PPI and
Sigmoid-PPI. **The paper studies point estimation, measured by mean absolute error; the strings
"confidence interval", "coverage" and "CI" do not occur in it, and it defines no interval for its
proposed estimators.** It is therefore not cited as prior work on interval coverage, and must not
be represented as such.

**[9b] Hsu (1938) — PARTIAL, and the manuscript says so.** *Statistical Research Memoirs* volume 2
is not available online and **we did not read it.** The bibliographic string and the content of the
result — the stochastic domination giving the `t_{min(f₁,f₂)}` bound — are taken from the reference
list and Lemma 2 of Wang & Liu (2026), which we did read. It is cited only for a result we
explicitly disclaim, alongside Mickey & Brown (1966), which we read in full and which proves the
two-sided version analytically. No claim in this paper depends on Hsu being read.

**[9c] Wang & Liu (2026) — read, but a preprint.** Retrieved and read as HTML full text
(arXiv:2606.07847, submitted 5 June 2026); **not peer-reviewed**. Cited for two results we
disclaim: their Theorem 1 (`t_min` is the shortest critical value available to a procedure that
does not use the variance ratio) and their Theorem 4 (an adaptive rule can only redistribute width
across variance-ratio regimes). Both are theirs; see `docs/PRIOR_ART_GATE5.md`.

**[7] Sitter (1997) — PARTIAL, and the manuscript says so.** The paper is closed-access; OpenAlex
reports no open-access location and Crossref carries no abstract. **We did not read it.** Its
bibliographic record is confirmed, and its content is characterised in §2.4 from two reviews we did
read — Fuller (1998) and a *Journal of Official Statistics* paper on jackknife variance estimation
under two-phase complex designs. §2.4 carries an explicit blockquote stating this, and §10 repeats
it as limitation 8. **Nothing in the manuscript implies the paper was read**, and the assessment it
supports — that the two-phase literature contains no finite-sample interval theory — is marked
PARTIAL wherever it appears.

**[9] Mickey & Brown (1966) — the source of the conservativeness result.** Obtained from Project
Euclid as a scanned document and read page by page. Their Theorem is quoted verbatim in §2.5 and in
`docs/theory_finite_N.md` §V.1. **The manuscript claims neither their lower bound (conservativeness)
nor their upper bound (the cap on over-coverage); both are attributed to them at every mention.**

**[4] Mozer (2026) — the source of the PPI/GREG equivalence.** Read in full (18 pages). §2.3 states
plainly that we claim nothing on that axis. A full-text term search of the paper is recorded in
`docs/prior_art_findings.md` §4d and supports the manuscript's statement that the PPI literature
contains no finite-sample interval analysis.

**[10] Robinson (1976).** Read in full (nine pages, scanned). Cited for two things and no more: his
§4 tabulation of Behrens–Fisher coverage, and the fact that his own theorems concern relevant
subsets rather than coverage — a distinction the manuscript makes explicitly so that he is not
miscited as proving conservativeness. A published Correction (1982, *Ann. Statist.* 10, 321) exists;
we have not examined it and do not rely on anything it might amend.

**[6] Särndal, Swensson & Wretman.** The 1992 Springer monograph. Mozer (2026) cites a 2003
printing; both refer to the same work.

**[11] Welch.** Only the 1947 *Biometrika* paper is cited. An earlier 1938 paper is often cited
alongside it; we could not verify its record and have removed it rather than carry an unverified
entry.

**[17] Willett et al. (2013).** Cited for the Galaxy Zoo 2 data underlying the demonstration in §9.
The Galaxy Zoo portal also recommends Hart et al. (2016) for debiased classifications; our data does
not use the debiased tables, so that entry is not carried.
